# Behavioral Interview Story Bank

Most behavioral interviews draw from a small pool of themes. Instead of preparing 50 separate answers, prepare **six strong stories** and learn to adapt them.

## The six stories to prepare

| Story | Covers questions like |
| --- | --- |
| A hard technical or analytical problem you solved | "Tell me about a difficult problem", "a time you used data" |
| A conflict or disagreement you resolved | "a time you disagreed with your manager", "a difficult coworker" |
| A failure or mistake and what changed afterwards | "a time you failed", "biggest regret", "feedback you received" |
| Leading without authority | "a time you influenced others", "took initiative" |
| Delivering under a tight deadline | "pressure", "prioritisation", "competing demands" |
| A result you are proud of, with a number | "greatest achievement", "impact you had" |

## Write each story once, in four lines

1. **Situation** – one sentence of context (team, goal, constraint).
2. **Task** – what *you* were responsible for.
3. **Action** – two or three concrete steps, using "I", not "we".
4. **Result** – the outcome, ideally measured, plus one sentence on what you learned.

Keep each story to about 90 seconds spoken. Longer answers lose the interviewer; shorter ones skip the action.

## Mapping a question to a story

When you hear a question, pause for two seconds and ask yourself which of the six themes it belongs to. Then open with a one-line direct answer ("Yes, last spring I had to push back on a launch date…") before telling the story. The direct opening proves you understood the question.

## Practice routine

- Day 1: draft all six stories in the four-line format.
- Day 2: say each one aloud, record it, and cut it to 90 seconds.
- Day 3: have a friend ask random questions from a list and practise choosing the right story quickly.
